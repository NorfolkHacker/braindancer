# ACID FM GEN: genre research for the IDM / braindance space

Status: research notes for retuning the GEN genre knob. No code was changed.
Date: 2026-10-27.

**How to read the numbers.** Genre tempo ranges are fuzzy. Genre encyclopedias (Wikipedia
included) seldom give BPM, and the BPM sites mostly run automated analysis that often reports
half or double time. The track-level figures below come mainly from Beatport's per-track
metadata for Warp, Clone and Rephlex-adjacent releases. That is the most credible source
that could be reached, but Beatport could only be read through search snippets (direct
fetches returned 403), and some of its locale pages disagree with each other. Figures from
automated "AI-analysed" sites are marked as such. Where no source was found, the figure is
labelled as the author's estimate.

## 2. Per-genre notes

### 2.1 IDM / "electronic listening music" / braindance
- **Status.** The umbrella term. Wikipedia describes IDM as a loose label tied to early-90s
  ambient techno and "electronic listening music." It names Warp's *Artificial Intelligence*
  (1992) and the 1993 IDM mailing list as origins, with ambient techno, techno, acid house,
  breakbeat and electro as its stylistic origins. Rephlex's "braindance" is offered as an
  alternative name, possibly a dig at "IDM." Rephlex defined it as encompassing "the best
  elements of all genres" (Mixmag; Last.fm wiki). Treat *braindance* as a marketing umbrella,
  not a separate sound.
- **Phases.** Phase 1 (~1991–95) beats are modelled on house, techno and electro. A later,
  jungle-influenced phase (Skam, Schematic, Planet Mu, Mille Plateaux, Morr per Reynolds) has
  "more forceful, inventive drum programming." There is a third phase from about 2004.
- **BPM.** The centre is about 120 BPM, with most tracks between 96 and 132. Data: Autechre
  "Bike" 125 (Beatport; Attack Magazine also says 125), "Eutow" 98 (Beatport). Autechre "Rae"
  switches between about 126 and 63 every two bars (AE Pages wiki). Plaid "Quick Emix" is 128
  in C minor; Squarepusher "K7 Museum" is 125 in D minor (Beatport); Aphex "Xtal" is about 114
  (blog estimate).
- **Rhythm.** Mostly straight 16ths with light swing. Electro- or techno-derived kits play
  syncopated, not purely four-on-the-floor, kicks. Expect tempo or meter tricks (Rae's
  half-time switches), "microrhythmic sets" of very rapid events (Papavassiliou, cited on
  Wikipedia), granular glitches and time-stretching.
- **Sound roles.** Melancholic pads, FM or bell-like leads, plucky arps, analogue-style bass.
  Harmony: minor keys and modal (Dorian/Aeolian) tonality dominate the Beatport keys above,
  with some major-key "nostalgic" pieces.
- **Reference artists (internal only).** Autechre, Aphex Twin, Black Dog, Plaid, B12, μ-Ziq.

### 2.2 Drill'n'bass
- **Status.** Core. Wikipedia: a drum and bass subgenre created by IDM artists in the mid-90s,
  with "fast, irregular beats," "impossible-to-dance-to rhythms" and a "lightly parodic"
  relation to jungle. Shapiro calls it "double-time [drum 'n' bass]." Pioneers: Luke Vibert
  (*Plug* EPs, 1995), AFX *Hangable Auto Bulb*, Squarepusher, μ-Ziq *Lunatic Harness*.
- **BPM.** The centre is about 163 BPM, range 155–172. Data: Squarepusher "Beep Street" 161
  (A♭ major) and "Vic Acid" 165 (E♭ minor), both Beatport. Aphex "Come To Daddy (Pappy Mix)"
  is listed at 81 BPM (C minor), which is the half-time reading of about 162. That is
  mid-90s jungle tempo, not modern D&B's 172–176.
- **Rhythm.** Straight 16ths, no swing, but extreme editing. Expect ratchets and retrigs,
  32nd- and 64th-note snare rolls, chopped Amen-style breaks, sudden fills, pitch-shifted
  snares and half-time drops. The style is defined by density and variation, not groove.
- **Sound roles.** Sub bass and (Squarepusher) slap-bass-like lines, acid squelch, sweet pads
  and childlike melodies set against the chaos (the *Richard D. James Album* aesthetic).
- **Descendant.** Breakcore (160–200+ BPM, Amen breaks, odd meters such as 7/4 on Venetian
  Snares' Planet Mu records).

### 2.3 Acid
Three distinct things share this name.
1. **Acid house (Chicago, 1987–).** 120–130 BPM (MasterClass, Music Gateway and others; a
   German school lexicon's 140–170 is an outlier). 4/4, light swing, a single 303 line
   with accents and slides.
2. **Acid techno (UK/Europe, 1990s).** 130–150 BPM, centred around 135 ("130–140 during the
   90s"). Harder, straight and more percussive.
3. **Rephlex/AFX "acid."** Analogue acid played with braindance irreverence: *Analord* (2005)
   is described as "straight-up acid, dense but basic 303 bleeps." No reliable per-track BPM
   was found for *Analord*. The author's estimate is mostly 120–140, with 4/4 or electro-broken
   kits. Aphex "Polynomial-C" is 144 BPM in C minor (Beatport), at the rave/acid-techno end.
- **For GEN.** The current ACID 122–130 is the Chicago end only. A braindance-flavoured ACID
  should run about 122–140 (centre ~130) with near-zero swing. Allow electro-style broken
  kicks as an option.
- **Harmony.** Minor/Phrygian riffs on one or two chords; the 303 slide/accent pattern is
  the "lead."

### 2.4 Electro (Detroit / Drexciya / Rephlex–Clone "analogue electro")
- **Status.** Core. Wikipedia lists electro among IDM's stylistic origins. Drexciya released
  on Warp and Rephlex as well as UR and Tresor. Rephlex released a lot of electro (DMX Krew,
  Bochum Welt and others).
- **BPM.** The centre is about 130 BPM, range 124–138 (DMX Krew is sometimes up to 143).
  Data: Drexciya "Wavejumper" 128 (A♭ major), Dopplereffekt "Voice Activated" 131 (B minor),
  DMX Krew 132–143 (Beatport). Other Drexciya tracks are listed at 129 and 131 in F and E♭
  minor. Mixgraph gives DMX Krew a median of 127 from only 4 tracks, so it conflicts.
- **Rhythm.** Roland TR-808, syncopated kick (not four-on-the-floor), snare or clap on the
  backbeat, staccato and "more mechanical than breakbeats" (Wikipedia electro). Machine-
  straight, swing about 0–4.
- **Sound roles.** Square/saw analogue bass in 8th- or 16th-note sequences, vocoder, sonar
  pings, cold minor-key string or pad stabs.
- **The current ELECTRO range of 112–128 is too slow** for this lane. 112–120 is closer to
  80s hip-hop electro (Bambaataa, Mantronix) than to Detroit or Rephlex electro.

### 2.5 Breakbeat / breaks / rave hardcore
- **Status.** Core roots. Early Warp and Rephlex output (Aphex rave EPs, LFO, Nightmares on
  Wax) came out of breakbeat hardcore. Wikipedia: breaks range from 110 to 175 BPM.
- **BPM.** The author's estimate puts IDM-flavoured breaks (Plaid, μ-Ziq mid-tempo, Bola) at
  about 120–140. Rave/breakbeat hardcore sits at about 135–150 (Polynomial-C at 144 fits).
- **Rhythm.** Sampled or programmed funk breaks with mild swing (6–10).
- **The current BREAKS 126–140 with swing 6 is reasonable.**

### 2.6 Ambient (intelligent) jungle
- **Status.** A strong adjacent lane. Wikipedia: atmospheric / intelligent jungle emphasises
  atmosphere and melody over complex re-sequenced breaks (LTJ Bukem, Good Looking). IDM
  artists' jungle phase (μ-Ziq, Squarepusher's slower pieces, Aphex "Vordhosbn"-style melodic
  breaks) sits between this and drill'n'bass.
- **BPM.** Mid-90s jungle tempo of about 160–170 (author's estimate; Wikipedia gives no
  numbers). Pads and bass are felt at half time, about 80–85.
- **Rhythm.** Rolling breaks with light ghost notes, little editing, long pads and Rhodes-like
  chords. Jazz-ish harmony: maj7 and min9 chords.

### 2.7 Glitch / clicks & cuts
- **Status.** Core-adjacent. Wikipedia glitch: Germany/Japan in the 1990s, Mille Plateaux,
  Oval's *Wohnton* (1993) and *Systemisch* (1994). The earliest documented use of "glitch" for
  music is linked to Autechre. The *Clicks & Cuts* series (2000) codified it.
- **BPM.** Often beatless. When it is beat-based it overlaps microhouse and minimal at about
  110–125 (author's estimate; no source gives a number).
- **Rhythm.** Clicks, CD skips and DSP artefacts as percussion; stutter and buffer repeats.
- **For GEN.** Best implemented as a *treatment* (ratchet/skip probability, bit-crushed
  clicks) on the IDM, MICRO or AMBIENT templates rather than as its own tempo slot.

### 2.8 Microhouse
- **Status.** Adjacent. It belongs through Ghostly, Mille Plateaux and Perlon/Kompakt; Matthew
  Herbert, Akufen and Jan Jelinek.
- **BPM.** 115–130 (Wikipedia; Bandcamp Daily and 6AM agree), centre about 122–124.
- **Rhythm.** 4/4 with "clicks, static, glitches" instead of hats; microsampled voices.
  Noticeably swung, Perlon-style shuffle.
- **The current MICRO 122–128 is acceptable but slightly high and narrow.** 116–126 fits the
  sources better. Raise swing (about 12–16).

### 2.9 Ambient techno
- **Status.** Core, and arguably the original IDM sound: SAW 85-92 ("flagship of the emergent
  genre," SPIN), Black Dog *Temple of Transparent Balls*, B12 *Electro-Soma*, Global
  Communication.
- **BPM.** No source number. The author's estimate is 105–128, centre about 118; "Xtal" is
  about 114.
- **Rhythm.** Soft 4/4 or subtle broken patterns, using 909/303 (Wikipedia).
- **Sound roles.** Reverbed string pads, gentle 303, bell or FM melodies, major-7 and
  sus chords.
- **There is no current GEN entry for this lane; it is the biggest gap.**

### 2.10 Ambient
- **Status.** Core (SAW II, Global Communication *76:14*, Biosphere). Beatless, with a nominal
  tempo of 60–90 that only drives LFOs and arps.
- **The current AMBIENT entry is fine.** DRIFT (IMPRESSION 60–92, swing 0) is nearly identical
  to it and therefore redundant.

### 2.11 Downtempo / hauntology: the "tape haze" lane (BoC-like)
- **Status.** Core. Skam and Warp released it. Critics describe "dulled," "molasses-slow
  hip-hop beats" (Uncut; Encyclopedia.com) on *Music Has the Right to Children*, with
  "hip-hop-inspired rhythms" carried over from *Twoism* and *Hi Scores* (Wikipedia). Simon
  Reynolds and Mark Fisher's "hauntology" (Ghost Box, 2005–06) treats this band as a
  precursor. The textures are vinyl crackle, tape warble and watery delay.
- **BPM (Beatport unless noted).** "Aquarius" 85 (G♭ major), "Smokes Quantity" 99 (D♭ minor),
  "In A Beautiful Place Out In The Country" 121 (C major), "Olson" in D♭ major (no BPM).
  AI estimates: "Telephasic Workshop" 88, "Kid for Today" 90; "Roygbiv" 70 or 84 depending
  on the tool (AcousticBrainz gives 84, D major). The centre is about 88–95, with a range of
  78–100 and an occasional ~120 track.
- **Rhythm.** Hip-hop drums, a slow kick-snare backbeat, moderate swing, slightly lazy.
- **Sound roles.** Detuned (chorused, pitch-wobbled) pads and leads, simple major or
  modal-mixture progressions (major with ♭VI/♭VII), melodic bass, children's voices or
  samples.
- **The current mapping (BOOM BAP 86–96, swing 16) is close on tempo and feel.** The problem
  is the narrow range. (The name RETROBOC is the owner's own and stays, 2026-10-27.)

### 2.11a RETROBOC: the Boards of Canada style in detail (owner's follow-up, 2026-10-27)

The owner keeps the name RETROBOC for this style (decision 2026-10-27; TAPE HAZE dropped). Follow-up research:

- **Tempo.** Mostly 68–100, centre ~80–85: "Roygbiv" 80 (Attack Magazine's recreation sets 80), "Aquarius" 85,
  "Smokes Quantity" 99, "Left Side Drive" 74, "Cold Earth" 68, "'84 Pontiac Dream" 68 (Hooktheory transcriptions).
  Faster outliers exist: "Turquoise Hexagon Sun" ~121 (an AI-estimated figure), "Dayvan Cowboy" 128 in its drum
  section (its intro is beatless, transcribed at 168). Range for GEN: **68–100**, with a rare faster draw if wanted.
- **Drums.** Sampled, hip-hop-derived breaks played slow and loose (light swing), often through a sampler (Akai
  S1000 per Avid) and degraded: low-passed, saturated, with tape noise. Not tight boom-bap: softer, further back.
- **Pitch wobble ("wow").** The defining trait: the whole mix or the synths drift in pitch, from tape (cassette /
  reel-to-reel, per Wikipedia on *Music Has the Right to Children*) or from an LFO on pitch. Attack Magazine's
  "Roygbiv" bass: LFO to pitch at **0.75–1 Hz**, 15–20 % depth, free-running (not retriggered).
- **Detune.** Small, deliberate: oscillators **~3–13 cents** apart (Attack: 3 ct; GForce's "Everything You Do Is a
  Balloon": +0.12–0.13 semitones), plus rich chorus. Warm, not out of tune.
- **Filters and envelopes.** Mellow low-pass (Attack: cutoff ~1.6 kHz, light resonance), long releases (~2–3 s),
  pads that swell; tape echo and slow flanger/phaser, subtle reverb (KVR thread).
- **Harmony.** Modal and bittersweet: **Dorian** ("'84 Pontiac Dream" C Dorian), natural **minor** ("Cold Earth" A
  minor, "Left Side Drive" E minor with minor-seventh chords v7–iii7–iv7–i7), and **major with sus2** chords
  ("Dayvan Cowboy" A major, Isus2 the commonest chord). Open fifths / no-third chords ("Cold Earth" VI(no3)).
- **What GEN needs for RETROBOC** (piece 6): BOOM BAP's drum skeleton slowed and softened (68–100, swing ~14), a
  slow free-running pitch LFO on pads and lead (≈0.75–1 Hz, a few cents to ~20 % of a semitone), 3–13 cents of
  detune, mellow filter, long releases, chorus / tape echo on the bus, scales Dorian / minor / major with sus2 and
  m7 chords. The interim retune (names, tempo, swing only) can set 68–100 and swing 14 now.

### 2.12 Glitch-hop / wonky
- **Status.** A descendant that belongs. Warp signed Flying Lotus, Hudson Mohawke and Rustie
  (*Butter*, *Glass Swords*). Ghostly's Dabrye and Schematic-era Prefuse 73 are the glitch-hop
  ancestors.
- **BPM.** Glitch-hop is usually given as 80–115 (sources vary from 80–130), centre about 90.
  Wonky is around 120–140 per one explainer, but it often reads half-time at hip-hop tempo.
- **Rhythm.** "Off-kilter, unquantised beats," "out-of-phase beats and synths that wobble"
  (Wikipedia, BBC). Heavy Dilla-style swing, micro-timing jitter and garish synth leads.
- **This is plausibly what the BROKEN 80–100, swing 14 template was really approximating.**

### 2.13 Skweee (fringe)
Swedish and Finnish, mid to late 2000s. 80–105 BPM (The List). Slow funk/R&B rhythms with
squelchy mono leads and bass. It fits an FM/303 synth well but is obscure. **It is an optional
alternate, not core.**

### 2.14 Footwork / juke (descendant via Planet Mu)
Nominally 160 BPM throughout. Sparse kick, triplet and polyrhythmic hats, chopped vocals,
sub bass (NPR; McGill timbre project). It entered the IDM world through Planet Mu's
*Bangs & Works* (2010). **Optional alternate.**

### 2.15 Folktronica (fringe)
Four Tet, Caribou and Múm. Acoustic samples plus glitch edits, about 90–120 BPM (author's
estimate). Poor fit for an FM/303 device. **Reject.**

### 2.16 Broken beat / bruk (West London)
- West London, late 90s: Bugz in the Attic, IG Culture, 4hero *Two Pages*, Reinforced. There
  is a real link to IDM-adjacent Detroit through Carl Craig, Kirk Degiorgio and As One.
  Kirk Degiorgio appeared on Warp's *Artificial Intelligence*.
- **BPM.** Mixmag gives 120–130 and another explainer 110–130; Wikipedia has no number.
  Syncopated, choppy, avoids four-on-the-floor, jazz-funk and Latin elements, often vocals.
- **Verdict.** This is a sibling scene (nu-jazz, Co-op, Plastic People), not IDM or
  braindance. **The current GEN BROKEN BEAT at 80–100 matches neither real broken beat
  (about 120–130) nor its scene.**

| BROKEN BEAT | 118–130 (124) | 12 | BROKEN retuned up | Only if the West London style is wanted for its own sake. |

**Template work this implies:**
- (a) A drill/ratchet layer on top of DRUM & BASS.
- (b) Range retunes on IDM, ELECTRO, ACID and MICRO.
- (c) Detune/wow modulation for RETROBOC.
- (d) Micro-timing jitter for GLITCH HOP.
- (e) An AMB TECHNO mapping onto DETROIT or DUB TECHNO with a softer kit.

FOOTWORK is the only alternate needing a wholly new template.
